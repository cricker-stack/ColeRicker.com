Cut to the Rhythm: How a Dancer Became a Content Creator

By Cole Ricker · TikTok: @ricker.mov

Good content stops the scroll. Great content makes people feel something and then gets them to act. I’m Cole Ricker, a content creator, video editor, and marketer, and I build work that does both.

It started with a favor

I was 16 when a friend who makes music asked for help. He had the wonderful music skills but no social media presence, and we had a camera and the willingness to figure things out. So I started filming, editing, and posting promotional videos to get his music in front of people.

That favor became my passion. I learned that a video is never just a video. It’s a pitch, a first impression, and one piece of a larger strategy. Every cut, caption, and posting decision either moves someone closer to your message or loses them. Since then, I’ve treated every project as both a creative challenge and a marketing problem.

Beyond the timeline: my internship at CGI

My creative skills grew in a place you might not expect. As a Business Analyst intern at CGI Technology and Solutions Inc., I learned how a global consulting firm runs day to day, and I built skills that transfer directly to marketing.

Research that sharpens strategy. I built stakeholder profiles and researched the businesses CGI wanted to partner with. Studying a stakeholder before a meeting isn’t so different from studying an audience before a campaign. Both come down to figuring out what a group of people cares about and how to speak to them.

A real content campaign. I managed a four-week engagement push for CGI’s internal SharePoint hub. I created the graphics, built the posting schedule, and coordinated content with leaders across the organization. It was a scaled-down version of a social media role, with me in charge of creative production, consistent planning, and cross-team coordination under a deadline.

Rallying people around a cause. I planned an “Office Olympics” fundraiser for the charity group Foster Love, which raised almost $700. It gave me hands-on experience with event promotion and audience engagement.

Using AI as a creative partner. I built and fine-tuned my own custom GPT to support work across CGI. It consolidated recurring transcripts from Microsoft Teams meetings into an action-ready dashboard showing who was in charge of each topic, when things were due, time-stamps of when information was said, which information needed human review, and more. Leaders on two teams began using it, and the praise I earned showed me what self-directed initiatives can accomplish. I see AI as a tool that extends my thinking rather than replaces it, speeding up production while my judgment, taste, and creative voice stay in charge.

Confidence with senior leaders. I interviewed seven people across the company, from consultants to directors to a Vice President. I started out nervous. By the end, I was asking sharper follow-up questions and leading conversations with confidence, a skill I’ll rely on when pitching ideas to clients.

The secret ingredient: ten years of dance

Before I was editing videos, I spent ten years in competitive dance. I trained across nearly every style: tap, hip hop, breaking, contemporary, lyrical, jazz, musical theatre, and ballet. That range taught me far more than technique.

Rhythm. Editing is choreography. A cut that lands on the beat feels satisfying in a way viewers can’t always explain but always notice. Years of counting music and hitting marks trained me to feel pacing instinctively. I know when a scene needs room to breathe and when it needs to snap.

Discipline. Competitive dance meant working hard toward a goal and pushing past my limits, again and again, both mentally and physically. That stamina carries into long editing sessions and tight deadlines.

Adaptability. Dance also taught me to handle sudden responsibility and to improvise when plans change. Improv turns a mistake or a last-minute change into a solution. Whether a plan goes sideways or a client shifts direction, I stay calm and solve the problem.

What sets me apart

Plenty of people can edit, and plenty can post. Few combine a sense of what looks good, an understanding of what captures attention, and a trained feel for rhythm. Here’s what you can expect when you work with me:

  • Precision. I’m detail-oriented and finish tasks with care.
  • Listening. I take the time to understand your vision before I touch the timeline.
  • Efficiency. I respect your time and deliver without cutting corners.
  • Strategy. I think about the purpose of the content and the audience, not just how it looks.

Where I’m headed

My goal is to create video and promotional content for a sports team, music artist or business, marketing firm, or advertising agency. I do my best work when I can take a creative product from start to finish, and I’m looking to collaborate with artists, brands, and teams who care about quality. If you’re an employer, a fellow creator, or just someone who likes the work, I’d love to connect.

My work is only getting started, and I’m building it one frame at a time.
